<p class="page-intro">Most of my time goes into growing infrastructure, building products, and improving the systems I use to do both.</p>
<h2>At work</h2>
<p>I lead growth at <a href="https://routescan.io">Routescan</a>: partnerships, conversations with chain teams, and positioning explorer infrastructure around what people actually need.</p>
<h2>On the building side</h2>
<p>I'm working on <a href="/projects/#sea-temperature">Sea Temperature</a>, an iPhone app for exploring sea-surface temperature, and <a href="/projects/#thicc">THICC</a>, a tool for investigating token liquidity and market structure. Both keep bringing me back to the same product question: how do you make the limits of the data as clear as the result?</p>
<p><a href="/projects/#gm10">Gem Mint Strategy</a> connects my interest in graded Pokémon cards with onchain accounting. <a href="/projects/#eva">Eva</a> is where I explore identity, accountability, and economically active agents.</p>
<h2>The system behind the work</h2>
<p>I'm developing my <a href="/projects/#portfolio-os">Portfolio OS</a> to keep project context, decisions, and agent activity connected. The focus is supervision: spotting stale information, checking outcomes, and making it clear when something needs my attention.</p>
<p>I wrote about <a href="/the-loops-i-set-up-so-my-agent-system-doesnt-lie-to-me/">the loops behind the system</a>. The implementation keeps changing; the need to check what actually happened doesn't.</p>
<h2>Outside the product work</h2>
<p>Workshops, events, and educational material with <a href="/projects/#team1">Team1 Italy</a>. I like helping people get from an interesting idea to something they can try for themselves.</p>
<h2>Thinking about</h2>
<p>What makes a small product worth continuing. How to keep AI-assisted work accountable. And how to run several projects without letting the machinery become the work.</p>
<p>This is a <a href="https://nownownow.com/about">now page</a>. I update it when my focus changes.</p>

<h2>Sui prodotti</h2>
<p>Lavoro a <a href="/projects/#sea-temperature">Sea Temperature</a>, un'app per iPhone per esplorare la temperatura superficiale del mare, e a <a href="/projects/#thicc">THICC</a>, uno strumento per studiare liquidità e struttura dei mercati crypto. Entrambi mi riportano alla stessa domanda: come rendere i limiti dei dati chiari quanto il risultato?</p>

<p>Sto sviluppando il mio <a href="/projects/#portfolio-os">Portfolio OS</a> per tenere collegati contesto dei progetti, decisioni e attività degli agenti. Il punto è la supervisione: individuare informazioni superate, verificare risultati e capire quando serve la mia attenzione.</p>
<p>Ho scritto dei <a href="/the-loops-i-set-up-so-my-agent-system-doesnt-lie-to-me/">cicli alla base del sistema (EN)</a>. L'implementazione cambia; la necessità di verificare cosa sia successo davvero resta.</p>
<h2>Oltre ai prodotti</h2>
<p>Workshop, eventi e materiali didattici con <a href="/projects/#team1">Team1 Italy</a>. Mi piace aiutare le persone a passare da un'idea interessante a qualcosa da provare in prima persona.</p>

<p>Cosa rende un piccolo prodotto degno di essere portato avanti. Come mantenere verificabile il lavoro con l'AI. E come seguire più progetti senza lasciare che la manutenzione del sistema occupi tutto il tempo.</p>
